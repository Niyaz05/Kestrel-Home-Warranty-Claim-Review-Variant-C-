## 1. Bottom line

* **Accuracy is the wrong KPI.** In May-Jun 2026 "approve everything" scores 97.48% accuracy and stops Rs 0 of fraud.
  The model's own review queue scores 93.99% (it flags 40 claims a month as fraud, most of which are genuine). The 97% target cannot tell a useful model from a useless one: a realistic 40-a-month list scores below approve-everything.
* **The model works only when it has seen the new regime.** Trained through 31 May and tested on June it puts **15 of 40** reviews on real fraud
  (ROC-AUC 0.92). Trained before 1 May and tested on May it put **0 of 40** on fraud (ROC-AUC 0.24): it had never seen the post-1-May pattern. **Retrain monthly.**
* **The queue is roughly break-even, not a profit centre.** At the policy costs a review has to be fraud about 21% of the time to pay back the Rs 380 goodwill,
  and about 36% if the Rs 260 contact cost is charged to every review. June achieved 37.5%; May 0%.
* **The strongest signal is the outlet.** SP3160 (10 of 12), SP3232 (7 of 10), SP3318 (5 of 10), SP3129 (4 of 9), SP3319 (3 of 7) (post-May small claims) hold 83% of that fraud.
* **Evidence is thin.** 141 frauds in total, 22 in the June fold. Intervals below are wide and we do not hide that.

## 4. How it was validated

* **Rolling-origin by month.** For each month Feb-Jun 2026: train on everything before the month, score that month. No random splits anywhere.
* **Partner history is "as-of".** Training rows see only labels from claims at least 30 days older. Validation months see a **frozen snapshot at the month start**, exactly how the live service and the test file are scored.
* **Queue = top 40 per month**, the investigation desk's limit. Ties are broken randomly.
* **Intervals** are partner-clustered bootstrap (1,000 resamples of outlets), because claims from one outlet are not independent.
* **Selection caveat.** Hyper-parameters (depth 3, 300 trees, strong L2) and the 0.7/0.3 blend were set by hand after exploratory runs on these same folds. Treat the numbers as mildly optimistic. No early stopping on any evaluation fold.
* **Features** (22 numeric + 2 categorical): amount and its distance below Rs 2,000, days since purchase vs warranty, customer prior claims, photo, partner tenure and type, product family,
  and the outlet's as-of fraud history (all claims and small claims). Dropped: inspection fields (exist only for inspected claims), free text, serial repeat counts, hour/day, volume counts.

## 7. The money
* Stopped per claim reviewed, June: Rs 532 gross; Rs 294 after goodwill; Rs 34 after goodwill and Rs 260 contact cost.
* The Rs 260 "blended cost of a service contact" is ambiguous. We show both readings instead of choosing silently.
* Break-even precision uses the June average caught fraud (Rs 1,418): 21% (goodwill only), 36% (goodwill + contact).
* Expectation for a test month at 25% precision: 10 frauds caught = Rs 14,181 stopped, 30 false holds = Rs 11,400 goodwill. Net Rs 2,781 (goodwill only) or -Rs 7,619 (with Rs 260 per review).
* February-April (before the change, larger claims): net Rs 47,260 over 120 reviews.

## 8. Is it the newer partners?
* New outlets (onboarded since 1 Jul 2025): 8 of 46 with labelled claims have any fraud; 14 of the 60 listed have no labelled claims at all (11 were onboarded after the data ends). "Clean" for those means "no data", not "verified clean".
* Fraud rate on claims: new outlets 7.1% vs older outlets 1.0%. So Ritu's instinct has some support in the rate, and Meenal is right that most new outlets are fine: 38 of 46 new outlets with claims have no fraud.
* The five outlets behind most post-May small-claim fraud were all onboarded Nov 2025 - Feb 2026: SP3160 (2025-12-27), SP3232 (2025-11-26), SP3318 (2025-12-06), SP3129 (2026-01-19), SP3319 (2026-02-11). Concentration: the top 12 outlets hold 68% of all fraud.
* Onboarding date alone is not a usable rule: it would flag 60 outlets to reach a handful.

## 9. Smaller checks (read with care: June has 22 frauds, a change of 1-3 frauds is noise)
* **Ablation, June fold:** full model: 15 of 40 (AUC 0.92); no partner history: 9 of 40 (AUC 0.92); no amount features: 13 of 40 (AUC 0.90); no customer history: 13 of 40 (AUC 0.92).
* **Stale snapshot:** the test file is scored with a snapshot that ages up to 3 months. June fold with a snapshot 0 / 30 / 60 days old: 15 of 40 / 17 of 40 / 15 of 40 in the queue. No visible damage in this one fold.
* **Unseen outlets (May-Jun):** 33 claims from 11 outlets with no earlier labelled claims, 4 frauds. Too few to say anything about cold-start performance.
* **Simple outlet rule (no model):** re-inspect small claims from outlets with 3 or more confirmed frauds at the start of the month. May: 8 claims, 0 frauds (the wave had not shown up yet).
  June: 32 claims from 10 outlets, 10 frauds (31%), 45% of June's frauds, Rs 13,784.

## 11. Known weaknesses
* 141 frauds; June fold has 22. All intervals are wide.
* One regime-consistent fold only (June). May shows the model fails if it is stale.
* Hand-set blend and hyper-parameters (see section 4).
* The test period (Jul-Sep) is scored with a snapshot frozen on 30 Jun, and its true fraud labels will arrive 30+ days late.
* Fraud rate in the test months is unknown; the stated expectation assumes it stays near the post-May level.
